Inbox Triage for School Email

I'm learning that middle school comes with a much higher volume of emails than elementary. I had Claude read all of it, figure out what actually needed a signature or a date on the calendar, and hand me a checklist instead of a flood.

StatusLive
Impact~60 emails → 8 real to-dos, refreshed every school morning
ToolsClaude (Cowork), Gmail, Google Sheets, scheduled tasks

The problem

Between the teacher newsletters, the front office blasts, the after-school-program vendor, the lunch-account app, and the library, my kid's school inbox was producing several emails a day. Almost all of it was FYI. A few times a week, though, something buried in paragraph four of a newsletter was an actual deadline — a form due Friday, a book needed by the end of the month — and I only found out by accident, or too late.

The approach

I asked Claude to search my inbox for everything from the school and its related tools (the payment app, the fine-arts program's own portal, the library system) going back as far as the mail history went. Instead of skimming subject lines, it opened the full body of anything that looked substantive and separated genuine action items — sign this, return that, pay by this date — from newsletter filler. It also caught threads that looked open but were actually already resolved, so I wasn't left chasing things I'd handled weeks earlier.

What I built

Two pieces, kept deliberately separate:

  • An auto-refreshing dashboard: a running digest plus a deadline table, safe for Claude to fully regenerate every time since I never edit it directly.
  • A Google Sheet checklist I actually work from, with a Done column I check off by hand.

Then a scheduled task, running weekday mornings: it re-scans the inbox, rebuilds the dashboard, and messages me a two-sentence digest — only calling out anything new or due soon, instead of repeating the whole list every day.

The interesting bit

The hard part wasn't pulling deadlines out of email — that's the easy 80%. It was deciding what the automation was allowed to touch. The dashboard has no memory, so overwriting it daily is free. The checklist has my checkmarks in it, so I told the automation to never rewrite it — just flag new items and let me add them myself. Same data, two very different update rules.
